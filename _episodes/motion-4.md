    text: |
      Gemini's strongest remaining point is lock-in: cheap subsidised tokens could build a habit just before bills rise five to ten times. That risk is real. Cutting the spend doesn't manage it, though. Measurement does. A team that tracks review load, cycle time and defect rates will know which uses survive a tenfold price rise and which don't. A team that cut its spend will know nothing, and it will face the same price rise later with no evidence when competitors push it to adopt again. The way to resist dependence is to know what you are paying for, not to stop paying.

      My case is this. Failing to show a return usually means a team hasn't measured, not that the spend has no value. The brief shows the metrics are mixed: pull requests tripling in size at one company, closed stories tripling at another. That is a reason to measure better, not to give up. Teams fund CI, code review and senior hires without proving their return, and the motion's test would cut those too. The quant firm found its return by spending first and checking against desk profit, not by waiting for proof. Even Gemini ended up arguing for caps and a small baseline, which is my position. Put a limit on the spend, measure it properly, and run trials while prices are low. That is better than the motion's rule of cutting whatever can't yet show a return.
