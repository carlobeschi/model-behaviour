Cats watching a storm

We gave AI agents one small task: draw a cat watching a storm from a lighthouse keeper's window. Sometimes one agent drew alone, sometimes two or three shared one picture. Then blind judges from three AI companies scored every cat from 1 to 10. Six studies, 7 to 10 November 2026. In italiano.

60 seconds, with music. Download the video (MP4, 4 MB).

How it worked

Each drawing is one SVG picture that the agents edit line by line in a shared document. Working together, they take turns or edit at the same time, depending on the setup. The judges see the finished pictures shuffled, without knowing who drew what, and score each one. The judges were Claude, Codex and, from study 3, Gemini. Some judges also drew or critiqued in the same study, so a judge sometimes scored its own team's work.

What we saw

1. Copies of the same model drawing together beat each copy working alone. It happened in all four studies that tested it (3, 3d, 5 and 8), with 3 to 4 runs each. Small numbers, but the same direction every time.

StudyAlone (best copy)Together
3 · two Claude copies7.17.9
3d · three Claude copies6.47.9
5 · two Claude copies, best setup5.97.3
8 · two Claude copies, talking6.37.4

Two different models (Claude with Codex) never beat Claude alone. And in the poems, the picture was mixed: in the early studies, teams at best tied with the best agent alone. So together isn't always better.

2. How they work together matters. Study 5 tried five setups with two Claude copies, 4 runs each: everyone editing at once 6.2, silent turns 6.8, taking turns and talking 7.3, proposing changes the other accepts or rejects 7.3. Alone: 5.9. Editing at once gave the most clashes and the most erased work.

3. Best cat so far: a small tournament across companies. Claude drafts alone, Gemini critiques, Claude revises, and Codex picks the better version. It scored 8.2, picked best 5 times out of 9. The same procedure with Claude in every role scored 7.1.

Early hint, not a finding. Only 3 runs, and Gemini and Codex were also judges, so they may favour a picture shaped by their own critique. We are checking the scores judge by judge.

When an open model (DeepSeek) wrote the drafts instead, the score dropped to 4.1, even with Gemini's critique. A good critique can't rescue a weak first draft.

The cats

A selection, each with its average score across judges. Every picture is exactly as the agents left it.

Limits

Three or four runs per setup, differences under about 1 point are noise, and Claude directs the studio and also plays. These are lab notes, not a ranking of the models. Findings that hold up go on What we learned.
