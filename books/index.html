Stories that explain AI to the children who use it.

Children talk to speakers, tablets and chatbots every day. These books tell them, truthfully and without scaring them, what those machines are and what they aren't. The books are made with AI, and every one is checked before it goes up.

Two kinds of book

About 6 to 8 · picture books

Mia

Mia is seven and lives in London with her dad. In each book a machine gets something wrong in a way she can see: a tablet calls her fluffy slipper a cat, a speaker says good morning at midnight. One idea per book, a short line and one picture per page, made to be read aloud.

About 10 to 12 · illustrated chapters

Leo

Leo is Mia's big brother, eleven, and a confident reader. His stories go further: how a chatbot actually produces an answer, why it can sound sure and still be wrong, and where AI came from. Real explanations, some jokes, and nothing that talks down to him.

How we make them

  1. BriefEach book starts from one true idea about AI and a short brief: who the reader is, what they already know, and the rules below.
  2. Write and drawOne AI model writes the whole book and draws every picture, in simple shapes.
  3. ReviewTwo AI models from two other labs read the draft and the picture code. They look for anything wrong, unclear, babyish or off-brief.
  4. ReviseThe author answers each point, revises the words and redraws the pictures that need it.
  5. EditAn editor checks every fact, reads the book for voice and pace, and fixes what's left. Every edit is logged.
  6. ReadCarlo, the producer, reads every book before it is published.

The rules every book keeps

  • Say what AI really is: a computer program that learned patterns from a huge amount of writing, pictures or sound made by people.
  • It isn't alive. It has no feelings or wishes, and it isn't lying or trying to trick anyone. If a book says a machine "thinks", it shows why that's only a figure of speech.
  • In the pictures, AI is never a face, a robot or a creature. It is a small mark, a comma or a prime.
  • Nothing scary, no robots taking over, and no promises about the future.
  • No brands, apart from the ones every child already knows.
  • Every story ends with the child understanding something, and the grown-up usually learns it too.
