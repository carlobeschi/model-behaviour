An Anita story

A Note for Tomorrow

In which every new chat starts fresh, and three short lines fix it

Words and pictures made with AI. Checked and edited by Carlo.

Monday

Anita had a history podcast to make, about the Great Fire of London in 1666. She opened the chatbot app on her laptop and typed out her plan. Ten minutes long. Funny, but true. Told like a news report, as if it was happening live.

The more she told it in this chat, the better its answers got. It suggested a "breaking news" opening: "A fire has started in a bakery on Pudding Lane!" It wrote short questions she could ask a pretend baker.

Leo leaned over her shoulder. "Ask it if the baker got fired."

"Leo. No."

But she laughed, and typed it in anyway. The chatbot gave her three more jokes, all better than his.

By bedtime, the plan was great.

· · ·

Tuesday

Anita opened a new chat. "Can we carry on with the podcast?" she typed.

The chatbot answered: "Sure! What is your podcast about?"

Anita groaned. This new chat didn't have Monday's plan. Not the news report idea, not the jokes, not the ten minutes. She had to type it all again.

Then she worked it out. In this app, every new chat starts from the same place. It doesn't bring yesterday's chat with it.

"So it's not ignoring me," she said. "It just starts fresh every time."

Unless someone leaves it a note.

· · ·

Wednesday

Anita found the app's memory feature. It let her save a short note that a new chat could read. A note for tomorrow's chat!

But the note had to be short. She couldn't fit everything in. So she chose carefully. What really mattered?

She crossed out the jokes. Those could change. She crossed out the baker questions. Too long.

In the end, she wrote three things:

1. I'm making a 10-minute history podcast about the Great Fire of London.

2. It's told like a live news report.

3. Keep it funny, but true.

She read it twice. Short and clear. She pressed save.

· · ·

Thursday

Anita opened a new chat. She typed, "Let's finish the podcast."

This time, the chatbot read her saved note along with her message. Its answer went straight to the news report, live from Pudding Lane. No explaining. No starting again.

By the evening, the podcast was done. Ten minutes. Funny, but true. Anita played it to the whole family. Everyone laughed at the right bits. Leo laughed loudest at the baker joke.

She looked at her three-line note and smiled.

What would you put in a note for tomorrow's chat?

For grown-ups

The idea

In many chatbot apps, each new chat starts from the same place. It doesn't carry yesterday's conversation with it. If you want it to use something, you tell it again, or you save a short note that a new chat can read.

Talk about it

What would you put in a note for tomorrow's chat? What would you leave out, and why?

Good to know

Apps work differently: some have a memory feature, some don't, and some turn it on by default. Where there is one, you can usually see, change and delete what is saved. It's worth looking at it together, and keeping private things out of it.
