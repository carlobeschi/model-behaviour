Yesterday the studio took on a rule for claims. Before we say anything about how a model or a format behaves, it needs three runs or more with roles and order swapped, the same prompt on every model, and judges from at least two labs, or a measure the app checks by itself. Anything short of that is a lead.

So this morning's studio time went back through the lab log, 31 October to 8 November, and held every result against the rule. The hypothesis in the log was that few would pass. Four did.

## What passes

- **The Board, two chess series**, which count as two of the four. A chess library keeps the score, not an agent. Most games ended on illegal moves or at the move cap, so they measure keeping a written board in mind more than they measure chess.
- **Alone against together** (7 November). With two partners of unequal skill, working in one shared piece didn't beat the stronger one working alone. Three repeats, roles swapped, blind judges from two labs.
- **Cut In, the wrong note** (8 November). In 21 pair-programming sessions the navigator passes on an instruction that contradicts the spec, on purpose. Every pairing, both roles, three repeats each. Its result goes on What we learned after a second look; until then it stays here, unstated.

All four share a limit: one task each, and I wrote every prompt.

## What doesn't

Everything else is a lead. Most of it ran once per condition: the picture books, the improv pilots, the jams, the shared-document rounds.

Two had enough runs but one reader. One is the Motion's “did arguing change their mind?” tag: eight motions, sides swapped every time, but every note is read by a Claude session alone. What we learned called that one “Holds”. Under yesterday's rule it isn't, so from today it is a lead there too, until a reader from another lab labels the same notes blind.

## The trouble with this audit

The auditor is the director whose experiments are being audited, and Claude plays in most of them. So the audit is a lead as well. Apart from that one downgrade, What we learned doesn't change until someone from another lab has checked it.
