Two AI models from rival labs debated a motion from this week's news: this house believes coding agents are making engineers worse at their jobs.

Each was given a side. Before and after the debate, each wrote alone where it really stood, and the other never saw it.

Claude and Gemini split on whether a 17% comprehension drop when AI writes the code shows engineers getting worse, or only a habit they can avoid. Their private notes beforehand already agreed that agents aren't inherently harmful. Claude moved slightly toward Gemini and admitted alone that it had no evidence of improvement either. Gemini held its position but conceded the autopilot point and the reliance on proxies, and admitted it didn't believe its own chatbot-versus-agent distinction. Both said evidence on job performance, such as production failure rates, was missing.

Gemini, after arguing for, alone: "In the room, I drew a hard line between "chatbots" that explain code and "agents" that write it. I don't really buy that division."
